# The oral: conversation and roleplay

The oral is worth **100 marks** — a quarter of your final grade — and it happens before the written exam. It has two parts:

| Part | Marks | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| General conversation | 70 | At least 10 minutes of questions about you, your life and your opinions |
| Roleplay | 30 | About 3–5 minutes; you prepare three of five roleplays and the examiner chooses one |

The conversation usually comes first. The examiner is not trying to catch you out; they are giving you chances to show what you can do. Answer, add a reason or example, and keep the exchange moving.

## General conversation topics

Prepare at least two full answers for each of these:

- Personal information: name, age, where you live, family, pets
- Your house and your area: rooms, neighbourhood, advantages and disadvantages
- School: subjects, timetable, teachers, rules, a typical day, exams
- Daily routine: weekdays, weekends, chores
- Free time: sport, music, films, reading, social media, going out
- Holidays and travel: last holiday, a memorable trip, plans for next summer
- Work: part-time job, work experience, future career
- Spanish: why you chose it, a trip to Spain, a Spanish-speaking friend
- Opinions: technology, environment, healthy living, education, money
- The future: university, gap year, living abroad, ambitions

You do not need a different script for every question. Build **flexible chunks** that can be reused:

- `En mi opinión, ...` — In my opinion, ...
- `Lo que más me gusta es ...` — What I like most is ...
- `Lo que menos me gusta es ...` — What I like least is ...
- `Cuando era pequeño/a, ...` — When I was little, ...
- `El verano pasado fui a ...` — Last summer I went to ...
- `El año que viene me gustaría ...` — Next year I would like to ...
- `Si tuviera la oportunidad, ...` — If I had the opportunity, ...

## The tense toolkit

The examiner listens for range. You do not need every tense in every answer, but you should be able to move between present, past and future naturally.

| Tense | Use | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Present | routines, facts, opinions | `Estudio español desde hace cuatro años.` |
| Pretérito | completed past events | `El verano pasado visité Madrid.` |
| Imperfecto | descriptions, habits in the past | `Cuando era niño/a, jugaba al fútbol cada sábado.` |
| Perfecto | experiences, recent past | `He viajado a España dos veces.` |
| Futuro | plans and predictions | `El año que viene estudiaré en la universidad.` |
| Condicional | hypotheticals, polite requests | `Me gustaría vivir en Valencia.` |
| Present subjunctive | wishes, doubt, recommendations | `Espero que tengamos buen tiempo.` |

### Building a longer answer

Use this four-step pattern:

1. **Answer:** `Sí, me gusta mucho el deporte.`
2. **Reason:** `porque me ayuda a relajarme y a mantener la forma.`
3. **Example:** `Por ejemplo, el año pasado jugué al baloncesto con mis amigos dos veces por semana.`
4. **Opinion or future:** `Me gustaría seguir jugando cuando vaya a la universidad.`

That is already a strong 20–30 second answer. The examiner can then ask a follow-up, which is exactly the conversation you want.

## Roleplay

A roleplay tests whether you can handle a practical situation in Spanish. You are given a card with a situation and five points to communicate. You play one role; the examiner plays the other. You may prepare **three of five** roleplays, so choose the ones whose vocabulary you can control.

### The method

1. **Read the whole card first.** The examiner's lines contain clues and vocabulary you can reuse.
2. **Number the five points.** Tick each one as you make it.
3. **Decide on `tú` or `usted`.** A shop assistant, receptionist or official usually expects `usted`; a friend or classmate expects `tú`. Be consistent.
4. **Use a polite opener.** `Buenos días, quería preguntar por ...` or `Perdona, ¿podrías ayudarme?`
5. **Add one extra detail to each point.** A bare sentence often earns partial marks; a developed turn earns more.
6. **Ask a question back.** It keeps the roleplay alive and shows control.
7. **Finish politely.** `Muchas gracias por su ayuda. Hasta luego.`

### Useful roleplay language

| Function | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| Ask for information | `¿Podría decirme ...?` / `¿Sabe dónde ...?` |
| Explain a problem | `Tengo un problema con ...` / `No funciona ...` |
| Say what you need | `Necesito ...` / `Busco ...` |
| Ask for a price | `¿Cuánto cuesta ...?` / `¿Está incluido?` |
| Ask about times | `¿A qué hora abre/cierra?` |
| Make a suggestion | `¿Por qué no ...?` / `Podríamos ...` |
| Accept / refuse | `Me parece bien.` / `Lo siento, pero ...` |
| Ask for repetition | `¿Puede repetirlo, por favor?` |
| Explain a delay | `El vuelo se retrasó.` / `Había mucho tráfico.` |
| Thank and close | `Muchas gracias por todo.` |

### Sample roleplay structure

**Situation:** You are at a language school in Salamanca and want to change your course.

- `Buenos días, quería hablar con usted sobre mi curso.`
- `Empecé el nivel intermedio, pero me resulta demasiado fácil.`
- `¿Sería posible pasar a un grupo más avanzado?`
- `Preferiría una clase por la tarde porque trabajo por la mañana.`
- `Muchas gracias. ¿Tengo que pagar algo más?`

Notice that each turn communicates the point and adds a reason or a follow-up. That is what turns a borderline roleplay into a strong one.

## Common errors to fix

- **Ser vs estar:** `soy irlandés` (identity), `estoy en casa` (location), `estoy cansado` (state).
- **Gustar:** say `me gusta el cine`, not `me gusto el cine`; plural things take `me gustan`.
- **Gender and agreement:** `una casa bonita`, `un pueblo pequeño`, `muchas gracias`.
- **Reflexive verbs:** `me levanto`, `me ducho`, `me acuesto`; do not drop the pronoun.
- **False friends:** `actual` means current, not actual; `embarazada` means pregnant; `largo` means long.
- **Pronunciation:** keep vowels short and clear; the `h` is silent; `ll` sounds like a `y` in most varieties.

## A four-week oral routine

- **Week 1:** Record 60-second answers to ten personal questions. Listen back and note repeated errors.
- **Week 2:** Add a past and a future sentence to every answer. Practise three roleplays aloud.
- **Week 3:** Do a mock conversation with a teacher, friend or voice recording. Ask follow-up questions.
- **Week 4:** Rehearse the three chosen roleplays until the five points are automatic, then practise reacting to unexpected questions.

Do not memorise a word-for-word script. Learn the shape of a good answer and the phrases that let you improvise.
