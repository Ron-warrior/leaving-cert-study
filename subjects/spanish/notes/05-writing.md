# Writing: opinion, dialogue, letter, diary and note

The written paper gives you four writing tasks in Sections B and C. Each has a different job, and each is marked for **communication first, language second**. Learn the shape of each task so you can spend your thinking time on Spanish, not on format.

## Section B Question 5: the opinion piece

**50 marks · 80–150 words · in Spanish**

You are given three statements and choose one. The marks are split between **content/communication (25)** and **language (25)**.

### Structure

| Part | Function | Useful language |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Introduction | State the topic and your position | `Hoy en día, ...` / `En mi opinión, ...` |
| Reason 1 | Explain why, with an example | `En primer lugar, ...` / `por ejemplo` |
| Reason 2 | Add a different reason or consequence | `Además, ...` / `por lo tanto` |
| Counterpoint | Acknowledge the other side | `Sin embargo, hay que reconocer que ...` |
| Conclusion | Restate your position | `En conclusión, ...` |

### Model opinion piece

**Statement:** `Es importante aprender cosas nuevas.`

> Hoy en día vivimos en un mundo que cambia constantemente, por lo que aprender cosas nuevas es fundamental. En mi opinión, nunca debemos dejar de aprender, tanto dentro como fuera de la escuela.
>
> En primer lugar, aprender una nueva habilidad nos ayuda a adaptarnos al mundo laboral. Por ejemplo, saber usar la tecnología o hablar otro idioma puede abrir muchas puertas. Además, aprender algo nuevo mejora la confianza en nosotros mismos y nos mantiene motivados.
>
> Sin embargo, entiendo que la gente tiene poco tiempo libre y que algunas actividades resultan caras. Por eso, creo que el aprendizaje no siempre tiene que ser formal: leer un libro o cocinar una receta nueva también cuenta.
>
> En conclusión, aprender cosas nuevas es una inversión en nuestro futuro y en nuestra felicidad.

This is about 130 words and uses present, infinitives, a modal structure (`puede abrir`), a connective chain and a counterpoint. It does not need a subjunctive in every sentence.

### Content checklist

- Address the statement directly.
- Give at least two reasons.
- Include a concrete example.
- Show awareness of the other side.
- Keep to 80–150 words; being far under loses content, far over risks language errors and time.

## Section C Question 1: dialogue or letter/email

**30 marks · choose one task**

### Dialogue construction

You complete **five of six turns** in a conversation. Each turn is worth **6 marks**. The other speaker's lines give you the context and often the vocabulary.

Method:

1. Read the whole dialogue, including the examiner's side.
2. Identify the five points you must communicate.
3. Write a full Spanish sentence for each point, not a literal fragment.
4. Add a reason, detail or follow-up question when the turn allows it.
5. Keep the register consistent: `tú` with a friend, `usted` with an official.

Example prompt:

> **Recepcionista:** Buenos días, ¿en qué puedo ayudarle?
>
> **Tú:** Say you have a reservation for two nights and ask if breakfast is included.

Strong answer:

> `Buenos días. Tengo una reserva para dos noches a nombre de Murphy y quería saber si el desayuno está incluido.`

The answer communicates both points and expands with a natural follow-up. A bare `Tengo una reserva` may earn partial marks.

### Letter/email

You write a formal or semi-formal letter/email making **five developed points** from eight suggestions. Each point is worth **6 marks**.

Structure:

| Part | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| Greeting | `Estimado/a señor/a:` / `Hola, ...` |
| Opening | `Le escribo para ...` / `Me pongo en contacto con usted para ...` |
| Body | Five points, each with a reason or example |
| Closing | `Muchas gracias por su atención.` |
| Sign-off | `Atentamente,` / `Un saludo,` |

Do not simply list the points. Develop each one: explain, give a reason, or say what you would like to happen.

## How the examiner bands the writing

The published schemes use broad bands rather than a checklist of every possible sentence. Knowing the bands helps you decide what to prioritise when you are short of time.

### Section B Question 5 — 50 marks

| Band | Content/communication (25) | Language (25) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Top | 18–25: clear argument, coherent, relevant, communicative intention fulfilled | 18–25: idiomatic Spanish, good vocabulary, few errors, correct tenses |
| Middle | 9–17: reasonably coherent, comprehensible, some irrelevant material | 9–17: adequate vocabulary, verbs generally correct, some errors |
| Bottom | 0–8: incoherent or largely irrelevant, intention not fulfilled | 0–8: limited vocabulary, most verbs incorrect, many errors |

If content scores zero because the answer is totally irrelevant, language scores zero too. Phrases copied directly from the text earn no marks.

### Section C Question 1 — 30 marks

Each of the five points is marked out of 6:

- **6:** intention fulfilled; verbs must be correct for full marks.
- **4–5:** intention more or less fulfilled; verbs generally correct.
- **2–3:** some intention fulfilled; quite a few errors or incorrect tenses.
- **1:** very small part of the intention fulfilled.
- **0:** no communication of the message.

### Section C Question 2 — 20 marks

Each of the four points is marked out of 5:

- **5:** intention fulfilled; verbs correct.
- **4:** intention more or less fulfilled; verbs generally correct.
- **3:** intention more or less fulfilled; a few grammatical or spelling errors.
- **2:** some intention fulfilled; quite a few errors.
- **1:** very small amount of message conveyed.
- **0:** message not communicated.

The same mistake is not penalised twice in Section C, but verbs must be correct for the top band. This is why tense control matters more than decorative vocabulary.

## Section C Question 2: diary or note

**20 marks · choose one task**

### Diary entry

You write a personal diary entry making **four of five points**. Each point is worth **5 marks**.

A diary entry should feel personal:

- Use the **pretérito** for what happened and the **imperfecto** for background and feelings.
- Include emotions: `me lo pasé genial`, `estaba emocionado/a`, `me sentí un poco nervioso/a`.
- Use time expressions: `Primero`, `Después`, `Más tarde`, `Al final`.
- Finish with a reflection or plan.

Model opening:

> `Querido diario: ¡Qué día tan increíble! Esta mañana me levanté temprano porque ...`

### Note

You leave a short note for a friend, host, receptionist or family member, making **four of five points**. Each point is worth **5 marks**.

A note is practical and brief:

- Address the person: `Ana:`, `Hola, Carlos:`
- State the purpose immediately.
- Use future, present or commands: `Voy a ...`, `He dejado ...`, `No te olvides de ...`
- Say where you are or when you will return.
- Close warmly: `Hasta luego`, `Un abrazo`, `Gracias`.

Model note:

> `Hola, Ana: Me he ido al supermercado porque no había pan. He dejado las llaves en la cocina. Vuelvo sobre las seis. ¿Puedes preparar la mesa para la cena? ¡Gracias! Un abrazo, Ciara.`

## Accuracy checklist

Before you hand up any writing task:

- Does every verb agree with its subject?
- Are past tense endings correct, especially `-ó` vs `-o`?
- Do adjectives agree in gender and number?
- Are reflexive pronouns present where needed?
- Did you use the required language (Spanish for production tasks)?
- Is the word count inside the limit?
- Did you answer every required point?

## Sentence upgrades

| Simple sentence | Stronger version |
| --- | --- |
| `Me gusta viajar.` | `Me encanta viajar porque me permite descubrir nuevas culturas.` |
| `Fui a España.` | `El verano pasado fui a España con mi familia y lo pasé genial.` |
| `Es importante reciclar.` | `Es importante que reciclemos para proteger el medio ambiente.` |
| `Tengo muchos amigos.` | `Tengo muchos amigos, aunque mi mejor amiga se llama Laura.` |
| `Quiero estudiar medicina.` | `Me gustaría estudiar medicina porque quiero ayudar a la gente.` |

The upgrade does not need to be complicated. It needs to add a reason, an example or a consequence — the things that earn communication marks.
