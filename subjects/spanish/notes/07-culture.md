# Culture and the prescribed text

Cultural awareness is not a separate exam paper; it is built into the reading texts, the oral conversation and the writing prompts. You do not need to memorise a list of facts about every Spanish-speaking country. You need enough knowledge to say something specific, compare with Ireland and give an opinion.

## Why culture earns marks

An answer that says `España es un país muy interesante` is grammatically fine but says very little. A stronger answer names a place, a custom or a current issue and explains why it matters:

> `En el sur de España es típico tomar tapas por la tarde. Me parece una costumbre muy social porque la gente comparte platos y pasa más tiempo juntos.`

The second answer adds a detail, a reason and an opinion. That is what the examiner is listening for.

## The Spanish-speaking world

Spanish is an official language in Spain and in most of Latin America, as well as in Equatorial Guinea and Puerto Rico. Mexico has the largest number of Spanish speakers; Spain, Colombia, Argentina and the United States also have very large Spanish-speaking populations.

Avoid treating the Spanish-speaking world as one culture. Food, music, festivals, accents and daily customs vary enormously between Madrid, Seville, Mexico City, Buenos Aires and Havana.

### Useful cultural comparisons

| Topic | Spain | Latin America | Ireland |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Meal times | Late lunch and dinner | Varies by country; often later than Ireland | Earlier dinner |
| Greetings | Two kisses common in many areas | A handshake or one kiss depending on country | Handshake or hug |
| Festivals | Semana Santa, Fallas, Feria de Abril | Día de los Muertos, Carnaval, local fiestas | St Patrick's Day, local festivals |
| Transport | Extensive train and metro networks in cities | Buses and metro systems; long-distance travel differs | Bus and rail network |

Do not present these as rules for every person. Use `en general`, `en muchas zonas`, `depende de la región`.

## Customs and daily life

- **Mealtimes:** lunch is often the main meal in Spain, and dinner can be late. In Latin America, meal times vary widely.
- **Siesta:** a traditional afternoon rest, especially in smaller towns and in summer. Not everyone takes one, and it is less common in big cities.
- **Tapas:** small plates shared with drinks, especially in Spain. In some regions, a tapa comes free with a drink.
- **Festivals:** religious festivals, local patron-saint days and carnivals are important in many communities. They can be deeply local rather than national.
- **Family:** family life and extended-family contact are often strong, but do not generalise about every household.
- **Football:** a shared cultural reference across Spain and much of Latin America, but also a source of regional rivalry.

## The prescribed literature option

In Section A, Question 1, you can choose the **prescribed literature** option or the **journalistic text** option. The literature option is worth 50 marks and asks detailed questions about the prescribed text.

Recent prescribed texts have included **Relato de un náufrago** (2024 and 2025) and **El medallón perdido** (2026). The prescribed text can change, so check the current SEC list and your teacher's guidance. Do not assume either text will be prescribed in a later year.

### How to study the literature option

1. **Know the plot in order.** Be able to retell the beginning, turning points and ending in five sentences.
2. **Know the characters.** For each main character, write their role, two adjectives, a relationship and one key moment.
3. **Know the themes.** Typical themes include family, identity, memory, growing up, friendship, loss, justice and the past.
4. **Learn short quotations.** Choose a few short, useful lines for each main theme. Learn the Spanish accurately.
5. **Practise the question types.** You may be asked to locate a quotation, explain what it shows, describe a character, discuss a theme or give your opinion.
6. **Answer in the required language.** Some questions may ask for Spanish; others may allow English. Follow the instruction exactly.

### Literature answer formula

- **Point:** name the character, theme or moment.
- **Evidence:** refer to a specific event or short quotation.
- **Explanation:** say what it shows.
- **Effect:** say why it matters to the story or the reader.

Example:

> `El medallón representa el pasado familiar del protagonista. Cuando lo encuentra, comprende mejor la historia de su familia y empieza a aceptar su propia identidad.`

Even if the examiner asks in English, a clear point-evidence-explanation structure works.

## The journalistic text option

If you choose the journalistic text, you are reading a real-world article, often about a social issue. The questions may ask you to:

- find synonyms in the text
- explain phrases in English
- identify the writer's opinion
- summarise an argument
- answer comprehension questions in Spanish or English

The skills are the same as Section B reading. The topic is often current: technology, environment, tourism, health, education, inequality or culture.

### Before you choose

- Choose the option you have actually prepared.
- If you have not studied the literature thoroughly, do not choose it just because it looks shorter.
- If you choose the journalistic text, read the questions first and locate the paragraphs they refer to.

## Current issues you can discuss

You do not need to be an expert, but two or three specific examples make an oral or written answer stronger.

### Technology and social media

- Advantages: connection, access to information, online learning, remote work.
- Disadvantages: misinformation, screen time, cyberbullying, privacy, addiction.
- Useful phrase: `Las redes sociales nos permiten comunicarnos, pero también pueden crear dependencia.`

### Environment

- Issues: climate change, pollution, waste, water shortages, loss of biodiversity.
- Solutions: recycling, public transport, renewable energy, reducing consumption.
- Useful phrase: `Es urgente que tomemos medidas para proteger el planeta.`

### Tourism

- Advantages: jobs, income, cultural exchange, investment in infrastructure.
- Disadvantages: overcrowding, rising rents, environmental damage, seasonal work.
- Useful phrase: `El turismo puede ser positivo para la economía, pero debe ser sostenible.`

### Education

- Issues: exam pressure, access, class size, technology in the classroom, mental health.
- Useful phrase: `La educación debería prepararnos para la vida, no solo para los exámenes.`

### Health and sport

- Issues: sedentary lifestyles, fast food, mental health, access to sport.
- Useful phrase: `Hacer deporte no solo mejora el cuerpo, sino también la mente.`

## Cultural sentence bank

| Function | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| In Spain it is common to ... | `En España es habitual ...` |
| In my region ... | `En mi región ...` |
| Compared with Ireland ... | `En comparación con Irlanda ...` |
| It depends on the region | `Depende de la región` |
| I find it interesting that ... | `Me parece interesante que ...` |
| It is a tradition that ... | `Es una tradición que ...` |
| In my experience ... | `En mi experiencia ...` |
| I would like to learn more about ... | `Me gustaría aprender más sobre ...` |

Use these phrases to move from a general statement to a specific example. That is the difference between a cultural answer that fills time and one that earns credit.
