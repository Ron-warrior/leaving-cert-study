# Higher Level Spanish: the exam in one page

Spanish at Higher Level is marked out of **400**. The oral is examined earlier in the year; the written paper and the aural are sat in June. The marks are split like this:

| Component | Marks | Weighting | Format |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Oral | 100 | 25% | About 15 minutes: general conversation (70) and one roleplay (30) |
| Aural | 80 | 20% | About 40 minutes: seven sections, each heard three times |
| Written | 220 | 55% | 2 hours 30 minutes: Sections A, B and C |
| **Total** | **400** | **100%** | |

The written paper is the biggest single block, but the oral is already banked before the written exam. A strong oral can take pressure off the June papers, so do not leave oral preparation until the last week.

## The written paper

| Section | Marks | What you do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | 70 | Question 1 (50): prescribed literature **or** journalistic text. Question 2 (20): two short reading comprehensions. |
| B | 100 | Questions 1–4 (50): vocabulary, synonyms, explaining phrases and comprehension work on a longer text. Question 5 (50): an opinion piece of 80–150 words in Spanish. |
| C | 50 | Question 1 (30): a dialogue **or** a formal letter/email. Question 2 (20): a diary entry **or** a note. |

You answer **all three sections**. In Section A, choose either the literature option or the journalistic-text option for Question 1; in Section C, choose one task from Question 1 and one from Question 2.

### What the examiner rewards

- **Communication first.** A clear message with a few small errors beats a complicated answer that says nothing.
- **Correct verbs.** In Section C, verbs must be correct for full marks on a point. Tense control is the single highest-value grammar skill.
- **Range.** Show more than one tense, a few complex structures and topic vocabulary that actually fits the question.
- **Accuracy.** Watch gender, number, accents and spelling. The same mistake is not penalised twice in Section C, but repeated errors still cost marks.
- **Relevance.** Answer the task asked, not a prepared essay that only loosely fits.

### A timing plan for the 2 hours 30 minutes

The exact paper varies, but a safe plan is:

| Time | Task |
| --- | --- |
| 0–30 | Section A, Question 1 (50 marks) |
| 30–50 | Section A, Question 2 (20 marks) |
| 50–75 | Section B, Questions 1–4 (50 marks) |
| 75–105 | Section B, Question 5 opinion piece (50 marks) |
| 105–125 | Section C, Question 1 dialogue or letter (30 marks) |
| 125–140 | Section C, Question 2 diary or note (20 marks) |
| 140–150 | Check verbs, endings, accents and word count |

If a comprehension takes longer, borrow time from the opinion piece rather than rushing the final Section C tasks. The short writing tasks are quick marks when you know the format.

## The oral

The oral is worth **100 marks**: **70 for general conversation** and **30 for roleplay**. The conversation lasts at least ten minutes and covers familiar topics — family, home, area, school, routine, free time, holidays, work experience, plans and opinions. The roleplay lasts about three to five minutes. For the current arrangements you prepare **three of five roleplays**, and the examiner chooses one.

The oral is not a memory test. Examiners reward students who can keep a conversation going, react to a follow-up question and use a range of tenses. Learn a bank of flexible sentences, not a script.

## The aural

The listening paper is worth **80 marks**. It has seven sections, and each section is heard **three times**, with a pause during the second hearing. Expect announcements, descriptions, dialogues, weather, news, interviews and short reports. Read the questions before the first hearing and use the three hearings deliberately: gist first, detail second, check third. **Answers are in English**; the SEC schemes award half marks if the whole test or section is answered in Spanish.

## Five habits that move the grade

1. **Learn verbs in patterns.** Pick five high-frequency verbs and write them in the present, pretérito, imperfecto, perfecto, futuro and condicional. Then use them in your own sentences.
2. **Build a sentence bank by topic.** For each oral or writing topic, learn five opinions, two past examples and two future plans.
3. **Speak aloud every day.** A five-minute recording of one oral question is worth more than silently reading a page of vocabulary.
4. **Use past papers as training, not decoration.** Do one comprehension under time, then correct it against the scheme and list the exact reason you lost marks.
5. **Follow the Spanish-speaking world.** A short news story each week gives you examples for opinion pieces and oral answers about technology, environment, sport, culture or current affairs.

## Before you start

The structure above follows the 2026 Higher Level paper and the current oral arrangements. The SEC can adjust assessment arrangements, and the prescribed literature and roleplay list can change. Always check the current SEC examination paper, marking scheme and roleplay list before relying on a date-specific detail.

Use the notes in this pack in this order:

1. **The oral** — build the conversation and roleplay toolkit.
2. **Listening** — train the aural question types.
3. **Reading** — learn how Sections A and B are marked.
4. **Grammar** — fix the high-frequency errors.
5. **Writing** — learn the opinion, dialogue, letter, diary and note formats.
6. **Vocabulary** — fill topic gaps.
7. **Culture and literature** — prepare the content options.
8. **Exam-style practice** — put it all together under time.
